Where MIS leads

12 career families

Each family groups jobs that use similar skills. Start with the entry-level titles to see what to search for.

Process Improvement & Operational Excellence

Finding the waste in how work gets done, and fixing it.

You map how work actually flows, find the delays and errors, fix the root cause, and measure whether it improved. In MIS this usually means processes that run on software, data and automation.

Entry-level titles

  • Process Analyst Intern
  • Continuous Improvement Intern
  • Operations Improvement Analyst

Business Analysis & Requirements

Making sure the right thing gets built.

Business analysts figure out what a business actually needs and turn it into clear requirements, user stories and acceptance criteria a tech team can build and test against.

Entry-level titles

  • Business Analyst Intern
  • IT Business Analyst
  • QA / User Acceptance Testing Analyst

Business Systems & Enterprise Applications

Running the software a whole company depends on.

You configure, connect and improve business platforms like ERP (company-wide operations), CRM (sales and customers) and HR systems, then help roll out changes so people actually use them.

Entry-level titles

  • Business Systems Analyst Intern
  • ERP or CRM Analyst
  • Implementation Analyst

Intelligent Automation & Applied AI

Taking repetitive work off people's plates.

You find work that can be automated, then build it with low-code tools, scripts or AI workflows, with the right controls and human oversight. This is about deploying AI in a business, not researching AI models.

Entry-level titles

  • Automation Analyst Intern
  • Power Platform Intern
  • RPA (robotic process automation) Analyst

Data Analytics & Business Intelligence

Turning messy numbers into decisions.

You clean, explore and visualize data so leaders can see what's happening and why. BI (business intelligence) work centers on dashboards and the metrics a business runs on.

Entry-level titles

  • Data Analyst Intern
  • BI Intern
  • Reporting Analyst

Data Governance & Information Management

Making sure data can be trusted.

You define what data means, who owns it, who may use it, and how its quality is checked. Data governance sits between the business and the database teams, and AI makes it more important.

Entry-level titles

  • Data Quality Analyst
  • Data Steward
  • Data Governance Intern

Governance, Risk, Compliance & AI Governance

Proving that systems are safe, fair and following the rules.

GRC (governance, risk and compliance) professionals assess technology risk, test controls, gather audit evidence, and make sure the organization meets laws and standards. A fast-growing part of this is governing how AI is used.

Entry-level titles

  • IT Audit Intern
  • GRC Analyst Intern
  • Technology Risk Intern

Cybersecurity Operations & Identity

Defending systems in real time.

You monitor for threats, investigate alerts, manage who can log in to what (identity and access management, or IAM), and respond to incidents. It's more hands-on and technical than GRC.

Entry-level titles

  • Security Operations Center (SOC) Intern
  • IAM Intern
  • Cybersecurity Intern

IT Project, Program & Product Delivery

Getting the right people to ship the right thing on time.

You plan the work, coordinate teams, manage risks and keep stakeholders informed. Some roles focus on delivering projects on time; product roles focus on deciding what gets built and why.

Entry-level titles

  • IT Project Coordinator
  • Project Management Office (PMO) Intern
  • Product Operations Intern

UX Research & Service Design

Making technology work for the people using it.

You study how people actually use a service, map their journey, test designs, and push for changes that make systems easier and more accessible. In MIS this focuses on research and service redesign more than visual design.

Entry-level titles

  • UX Research Intern
  • Service Design Intern
  • Digital Experience Analyst

IT Operations, Cloud & Infrastructure

Keeping the systems everyone depends on running.

You run and support the networks, devices, accounts and cloud services a company runs on. Many careers start in IT support and grow into systems, cloud or platform roles.

Entry-level titles

  • IT Operations Intern
  • Cloud Operations Intern
  • Service Desk Analyst

Tech Consulting, Strategy & Architecture

Helping organizations decide what technology to invest in.

You diagnose business problems, compare options, build the business case, and design how systems should fit together. Many people start in analyst or consulting roles; architect titles usually come later.

Entry-level titles

  • Technology Consulting Intern
  • IT Advisory Intern
  • Vendor Management Analyst

Next step

Landing an internship

Five practical steps you can start this week.

List your classes, team projects, and the tools you have used, such as Excel, SQL, or Power BI. Keep it to one page and use plain words.

Then get a second opinion. The CSUSM Career Center says its Handshake service offers resume and cover letter critiques. Visit the Career Center (opens in new tab).

Add a clear photo, a short headline that says what you study, and the projects and tools you have worked with. Then connect with classmates, guest speakers, and alumni.

The Career Center lists free headshots on its home page, so ask about them before you pay for a photo.

The Career Center says Handshake is where you can make appointments, search for jobs, and RSVP for events. It also lists career counseling, mentorship, and internship information for students.

Start at the Career Center home page (opens in new tab).

Ask a professional for a short chat about their work. Ask what they do in a normal week, which skills matter most, and how they got started. Say thank you afterward.

Not sure whom to ask about? Take the Career Fit Quiz above, then pick two or three career families from your results.
